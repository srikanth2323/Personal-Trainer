# PHA Training Plan

**Equipment:** Straight bar, EZ (zigzag) bar, plates 5-40kg, dumbbells 5-15kg, flat bench (no incline)
**Frequency:** 6 days/week — 3 workout days alternating with 3 walking-only (active rest) days, 1 full rest day
**Format:** Full-body PHA circuit each session, alternating Workout A / Workout B

---

## Why A/B Rotation

Since this is your *only* training long-term, one circuit repeated forever will leave gaps and cause staleness. Two alternating full-body PHA circuits (A and B) together hit every major muscle group — chest, back, traps, shoulders (front and rear delts), biceps, triceps, forearms, quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, and core — across the week, while each individual session still keeps the classic PHA alternation of upper/lower with minimal rest. Each circuit now runs 12 stations, with 2 of them bodyweight moves (push-ups/dips) that also work as built-in "active rest" — lower loading, but they keep heart rate elevated within the round.

**Weekly pattern:** Workout, Walk, Workout, Walk, Workout, Walk, Full rest — 3 workout days and 3 walking (active rest) days alternate through the week, with A and B trading off on workout days (e.g. A, B, A one week → B, A, B the next)

## PHA Execution Rules
- Minimal rest (15-20 sec) between exercises within a round — this is what makes it PHA, not standard strength work
- Rest 60-90 sec between full rounds
- **3 rounds per session** to start

## Progression

| Phase | Rounds | Load focus |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| Foundation | 1-3 rounds | Moderate loads, lock in form and pacing |
| Build | 3-4 rounds | Increase barbell loads (squats/deadlifts/rows toward heavier end of your plates); DBs toward 12-15kg on presses/curls |
| Established | 4 rounds | Push loads further where form holds; shorten inter-round rest slightly (60-75 sec) for more conditioning demand |

Add weight roughly every 1-2 weeks on an exercise once you can complete all rounds at the top of the rep range with good form. Move to the next phase whenever the current one feels comfortable — there's no fixed timeline.

---

## Loading Suggestions (starting points — adjust to your form)
- Squats, deadlifts, RDLs, rows, hip thrusts: load the barbell up, using your 5-40kg plates — you have plenty of headroom
- Overhead press, bench press: moderate barbell/DB load — shoulders and chest usually lag legs/back in relative strength
- Curls, lateral raises, skull crushers: 8-15kg DBs / light-moderate EZ bar
- Bulgarian split squats, lunges: 8-12kg DBs per hand to start (balance adds difficulty even at lighter loads)

## Safety Checkpoints
- Track how you feel each session — unusual breathlessness, chest discomfort, or dizziness beyond normal exertion means stop and check in with your cardiologist, not push through
- Every 6-8 weeks, do a quick self-check: are rounds getting easier at the same load? That's your progression signal
- If you miss more than 2 weeks (illness, travel), restart at Foundation-phase loads rather than where you left off
