# PHA Training Plan

**Equipment:** Straight bar, EZ (zigzag) bar, plates 5-40kg, dumbbells up to 10kg each, flat bench (no incline), pull-up bar (on another floor — not used in the circuit)
**Frequency:** 3 workout days a week, alternating with walking-only (active rest) days, plus full rest
**Format:** Full-body PHA circuit each session, alternating Workout A / Workout B
**Rounds:** **ONE round per session.** Your cardiologist advised 30–40 minutes; more than that is not more helpful. Each station is done once, back to back.

---

## Why A/B Rotation

Two alternating full-body circuits (A and B) together hit every major muscle group *and* the individual heads within them across the week — biceps (long head, short head, brachialis), triceps (long, lateral, medial), all three deltoid heads, upper/mid/lower chest, lats and mid-back, lower traps, glute max and medius, hamstrings as both hip-extensors and knee-flexors, gastrocnemius and soleus, forearm flexors/extensors, core — while each session keeps the classic PHA alternation of upper and lower body with minimal rest. Each circuit now runs 15 stations plus a short core finisher; the bodyweight stations (push-ups, dips, hamstring curl, hip abduction) double as built-in active rest.

**Weekly pattern:** Workout, Walk, Workout, Walk, Workout, Walk, Full rest — A and B trade off on workout days (e.g. A, B, A one week → B, A, B the next)

## PHA Execution Rules
- **One round**, stations back to back with minimal rest (about 20-30 sec between exercises) — this is what makes it PHA, not standard strength work
- Target total time **30–40 minutes** including warm-up and core finisher (roughly 1.5 min per station). If a session runs well over, shorten rests rather than adding rounds
- Sip water between stations; stop and check in with your cardiologist for unusual breathlessness, chest discomfort or dizziness

## Progression

With one round per session, progression comes from load and quality, not volume:

- Add weight roughly every 1–2 weeks on an exercise once you can finish the top of the rep range with good form
- Dumbbell stations are capped at 10 kg per hand: progress those with slower tempo (3 sec down), a 1-sec pause at the top, or more reps within the range
- Bodyweight stations: progress by more reps, slower tempo, or changing leverage (e.g. feet-elevated, deeper range)
- Barbell/EZ-bar stations: add small plate increments, only while form stays clean

---

## Loading Suggestions (starting points — adjust to your form and your cardiologist's guidance)
- Squats, deadlifts, RDLs, rows, hip thrusts, shrugs: load the barbell using your 5-40kg plates
- Overhead press: moderate barbell load — shoulders usually lag legs/back in relative strength
- Curls, reverse curl, overhead triceps extension: light-moderate EZ bar; the reverse curl and overhead extension should be lighter than your normal curl
- Seated calf raise: use the barbell across the thighs — 10 kg dumbbells are too light for the soleus
- Prone Y-raise: 0–5 kg only; it is a small muscle and form matters more than load
- Lateral raise, reverse fly, shoulder press, bench press, single-arm row: up to 10 kg dumbbells
- Bulgarian split squats, lunges: dumbbells in hand, whatever keeps balance and form

## Safety Checkpoints
- Track how you feel each session — unusual breathlessness, chest discomfort, or dizziness beyond normal exertion means stop and check in with your cardiologist, not push through
- Avoid breath-holding (Valsalva) on hard reps — exhale on the effort
- If you miss more than 2 weeks (illness, travel), restart at lighter loads rather than where you left off

## Ordering rationale

1. **Upper/lower alternation.** PHA shunts blood between upper and lower body, keeping the heart working without any one muscle failing. The longest run of same-region stations is held to two, and no muscle is trained back-to-back (the two push-up variants in A are separated by the hamstring curl).
2. **Heavy compounds early and spaced.** The most demanding lift comes first, while fresh. In Workout B the deadlift (1) and hip thrust (6) are separated rather than stacked.
3. **Barbell load order (known issue).** Barbell stations run roughly heaviest to lightest, with one exception: in Workout B, Barbell Shrugs (progressed to 40 kg) come after the lighter deadlift and hip thrust, so one plate change is needed. Moving shrugs earlier would pre-fatigue the grip before the deadlift, so it is left as is.
